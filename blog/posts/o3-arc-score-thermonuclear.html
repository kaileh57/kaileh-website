o3's 87.5% ARC Score Should Worry You

December 23, 2024

OpenAI apparently just casually walked into AGI territory last week and nobody noticed.

In amongst all their "12 Days of OpenAI" announcements, they casually dropped that their new o3 model got 87.5% on the ARC Prize benchmark. If you're not an AI expert, that means nothing. But what it actually means is they might just have invented something that can think.

ARC isn't a normal AI test. It's not about spitting out facts or recognizing patterns. It's just these simple visual problems. Like the ones in IQ tests. Look at these shapes, figure out the pattern and use it on this new example. Fairly easy for humans. Most AIs fail horribly.

The best models before this barely got over 50%. GPT-4 wasn't even close. Claude didn't stand a chance. The whole point of ARC is to make sure all the cheap pattern matching tricks that let AI seem smart today won't work. It has to be proper thinking. The ability to grasp abstract rules and apply them to new situations.

And now o3 gets 87.5%.

The whole AI safety community is seriously alarmed. And with good reason. That score is from a run at 172x the compute of o3's own high efficiency configuration (which got 75.7%). It exceeds the 85% figure for the ARC Grand Prize, but not the efficiency requirement that goes with it. And if they're playing by their usual rules... it's only just getting started. They're calling it "o3" because "o2" sounds like a British telecom. These folks are moving so fast they're running out of names.

But the real scary part? The rate of acceleration. Look at the AI performance jumps on key benchmarks just in 2024. The MMMU score shot up 18.8 points. GPQA was up 48.9 points. SWE-bench (a coding test!) up 67.3 points! In one year.

I've been tracking this AI race closely since ChatGPT came out. The progress curve isn't linear anymore. It isn't even exponential. It's a vertical line. One breakthrough just leads to the next one and the next. And it keeps getting faster. The more advanced models get, the better models we can build. Lather, rinse, repeat. That feedback loop is about to blow up.

The timing of o3 couldn't be more perfect. Terrifyingly so.

OpenAI hits us with this bombshell right before the holidays. People busy traveling, seeing family. Congress on break. Tech writers doing their year-in-review fluff. By the time we've wrapped our heads around it, they'll be on to o4.

And they absolutely will be. Likely within months.

Because that's the terrifying part. o3 isn't the finish line. It's just another step on the road. Towards something we haven't fully thought through yet.

What exactly is AGI anyway? Artificial General Intelligence. It's AI that can do anything humans can do, or do it even better. Not just in specific areas like chess or writing. The kind of intelligence that could learn new things as fast as we do. Reason through completely new problems. Even potentially improve itself.

We were told it was decades away. Serious researchers said 2050. Optimists said 2035. Doomsday types said 2030. But if o3 really did bust through the AGI barrier...

Then it isn't decades away anymore. It's next week.

And from here?

It's just a clear progression. All the predictable and alarming things that are going to happen next.

First? The world is going to tell us o3 isn't "really" AGI. Complain about all the things it can't do. All the ways it fails.

They'll just keep shifting the goalposts. Saying AGI is something different now. Anything to delay reality for a bit longer.

But OpenAI will keep building. o4 will get to 95% on ARC. o5 will do perfectly on whatever we set up to test. At some point, pretending it's not happening won't be an option anymore. By then it will be far too late.

I'm 17 years old. I thought I had my whole life to figure it out. College. Career. Maybe start a family one day. Is any of that even relevant? Why work so hard learning CS if AI is going to be a better coder than me before I even pick a major? Why even consider any career if AGI will do it better and cheaper than me while I'm still an intern?

My parents think I've lost it. They remember Y2K and all those doomsday prophecies that never materialized. But this is different. Y2K was a specific technical problem that had a clear solution. AGI is Pandora's Box. Once you open it, you'd better hope you can get it closed.

The thing that's infuriating is the deafening silence. No emergency Congress meetings. No grand UN statements. No Manhattan Project to stop the AI apocalypse. Just OpenAI casually throwing in the "we might have just made humans obsolete" bombshell between announcing ChatGPT's Santa Mode.

Am I misunderstanding this? Maybe o3 is just another incremental step and real AGI is still years off. Maybe the researchers in the labs will get alignment right before midnight. Maybe the government will actually realize what's happening and act before it's too late.

The facts speak for themselves. 87.5% on ARC. December 2024. We've crossed a line that we were supposed to leave untouched for another decade. Instead of taking a step back to digest it, we're pushing the accelerator to the floor.
