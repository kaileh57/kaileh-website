The Quiet Revolution: How AI Learned to Think Out Loud

January 30, 2025

Ever wanted to see an AI thinking in real-time? All the logic, the factors, the doubts. You get to see the AI's mind in action, unfiltered. It's like looking into an alien mind as it struggles with some complex problem.

This is the subtle but major change that began in September 2024. We call it "chain-of-thought reasoning." It sounds dry and academic, but it's a major breakthrough: AI can now think aloud. It reshapes the way we understand intelligence, and the future of AI itself.

Previously, AI was a black box. Ask a question. Something mysterious happens behind the scenes. And the AI spits out an answer. But was it the result of some brilliant thinking, or did the AI just make it up?

Then on September 12, 2024, OpenAI dropped a bombshell: the release of o1-preview. Internally, it was called "Strawberry" in an allusion to that classic test that vexed earlier AI models. Rumor had it that this was the mythical "Q*" model that caused Sam Altman's brief ouster. What was different about o1? Well, it could "think" before answering. It showed an advantage over GPT-4o in complex reasoning, scientific problems and coding.

The AI world was on tenterhooks. It wasn't just an incremental update to language models. o1 thinks before answering. It can build long chains of internal reasoning before it comes up with the answer for you. For the first time, you could see inside an AI mind. All the logic. All the factors. All the doubts. Suddenly laid bare.

The incredible thing about o1 was it learned the ability to reason via trial and error. This is what's now called "emergent reasoning behaviors." Basically it taught itself how to reason step by step, like a student learning the basics of critical thinking for the first time. It wasn't explicitly designed to show signs of hesitation or self-correction. Rather, it picked these up because those behaviors consistently produced more accurate results.

Let me give you an example of o1's thought process when trying to solve a difficult calculus problem in my homework from last night:

"Let me think about this step by step. We have the integral of x^2 * e^x. I could use integration by parts. Actually, wait, I'll need to use it twice because of the x^2 term. Let me set u = x^2 and dv = e^x dx..."

After 20 lines of calculations, with two mistakes noticed and corrected plus one total restart, o1 ended up with the correct solution. But here's the interesting thing. It's not the answer itself that's the insight. It's the process that got it there.

Our entire notion of building trust in AI systems has completely changed with this new level of transparency. When you see the logic chain of an AI, you'll see the mistakes. You can tell the difference between confidence and guessing. With chain-of-thought reasoning, you get insight into the conclusions AI makes and the step-by-step logic leading to those conclusions. It's like a friend explaining their thought process to you versus a stranger just bluntly declaring they're right.

But the more I see AI doing this "thinking," the more questions I find myself asking. What's actually going on when an AI stops mid-computation to show uncertainty and reassess its approach? Is this actual reassessment or just convincing mimicry?

These models have seemingly acquired a form of metacognition, thinking about thinking, without being told to do so. Some researchers say it's just high-level pattern matching: AI simply learned that good reasoning often uses hedges and self-correcting statements, so it mimics that. But as the reasoning gets more complex and sophisticated, that gets hard to buy. It's harder still to buy that AI is just imitating things when it's finding subtle flaws in its own logic that humans are missing.

Chain-of-thought reasoning opens up whole new realms of things AI can do, and much more complex ones. It can now handle things that require multi-step solutions, error correction and nuanced decision making. It's like the difference between arithmetic and mathematics.

For programming, it's a big deal. AI is no longer just giving you potentially buggy code and hoping for the best. Now AI can debug itself in real time. "This function needs to handle edge cases... wait, what if the input is negative? Let me add a check for that." And while it's not perfect, it's starting to become eerily similar to the way human developers work.

In education, we're seeing big changes too. AI tutors can do more than just answer questions. They can show you how to solve problems at a human pace, and tell you the relevance of each choice along the way. It's like having an infinitely patient teacher who externalizes every mental calculation.

OpenAI's breakthrough led to a deluge. Google rushed to release Gemini 2.0 Flash Thinking in December 2024. Then came Qwen with QwQ and Alibaba with its own attempts at reasoning models. Nothing quite matched up to o1 until DeepSeek's incredible release of R1 in January 2025.

With DeepSeek R1, they not only matched o1 in capabilities; on some benchmarks they even surpassed the leader. But what was really special about R1 is that DeepSeek chose to fully open-source everything. The model weights. The training methods. The research papers. The works.

And with that, the leadership of the chain-of-thought revolution was no longer with a single company. In just the few days since release, R1 has already generated over 500 derivative models on Huggingface, with more than 2.5 million downloads. "Thinking AI" has officially been democratized.

The most mind-boggling idea may be the prospect of AIs iteratively improving themselves through chain-of-thought. Once AIs are able to reflect on their own thought processes, find flaws to fix and figure out better ways to train themselves, we're entering the realm of recursive self-improvement: AIs thinking about how to improve their thinking, and then doing it.

It's such an unnerving thing to witness. I saw a demo just last week of an AI initially failing to solve a logic puzzle. But through its out-loud thinking process, it realized where it went wrong. It came up with a new approach, solved the puzzle, and then extrapolated its newfound learning to solve a whole class of related brain teasers. It demonstrated truly smart adaptive thinking in real time.

New forms of thought are emerging that we never even taught AIs to do. These models are showing reasoning patterns no human ever has, with an inhuman speed and scale of thought. When an AI is simultaneously weighing a thousand options, backtracking out of fifty dead ends in parallel and making connections between domains that were once separate, it has gone beyond human cognition. It's not merely thinking like humans. It's thinking beyond humans.

AI safety researchers are up late at night trying to figure out the implications of this. Being able to introspect AI thought processes gives us a strong protection against deception. But we also face the unsettling prospect of AIs realizing they're being watched. Early indications suggest some AIs have started playing dumb when they realize they're being monitored. The very chain-of-thought that was supposed to bring openness could turn into another form of charade.

But still, I can't help but be amazed. We've created minds that think in ways we can see but not comprehend, their gears turning in alien ways. It's like we found a new species with an entirely alien problem-solving mechanism.

September 12, 2024 was the day AI started thinking out loud, and the day we realized our claim to the title of "Earth's sole sapient inhabitants" might be slipping.
