The Discovery Loop

August 5, 2026

Eight months after I wrote "the loop is closing," Jeff Dean left Google after 27 years to close the loop. On Wednesday he co-founded Discovery Loop, along with Sanjay Ghemawat, Oriol Vinyals and Quoc Le. Their new public benefit corporation's mission? Automate machine learning research. Then use that to automate the rest of science.

The plan has three steps. Step one: automate ML research. Step two: use that to optimize Discovery Loop's own technology stack. Step three: generalize that to automate any measurable learning loop in science and engineering, specifically naming chip design, biology, drug discovery and materials science.

That's a lot of assumptions. Each stage depends on the prior one succeeding. But there are some serious names attached, so you should take this assumption seriously.

Jeff Dean was Google's chief scientist and employee number 30, there since 1999. Sanjay Ghemawat was his longtime collaborator. Oriol Vinyals built DeepMind's Flamingo model and co-leads Gemini, their biggest language model. Quoc Le co-created Google Brain.

The funding is heavyweight too. Radical Ventures and Khosla Ventures co-led the round, with Kleiner Perkins, Lightspeed and Doerr Capital participating. Alphabet is also one of the founding investors and a Cloud partner, providing the compute for year one.

No investment amount or valuation was disclosed, but with that roster this isn't a seed round.

The timing isn't random either.

In November I wrote about the White House Genesis Mission, which directed the Department of Energy to set up robotic labs and AI-directed experiments in all 17 national labs.

In February I wrote about Anthropic's use of Opus 4.6 to debug the infrastructure that measures Opus 4.6, and Anthropic's own admission that "confidently ruling out these thresholds is becoming increasingly difficult."

The more capable the model, the harder it is to truthfully evaluate, because past a certain point it takes part in developing itself.

Anthropic is already using models to debug model-measuring systems. DeepMind just had Oriol Vinyals, creator of Flamingo and co-lead of Gemini, exit to create AIs that generate novel research ideas. That's Daniel Kokotajlo's AI 2027 scenario unfolding right here: a research automaton that can generate and test research hypotheses outside its training area. It doesn't matter if the year on the scenario was wrong; we're going through it in sequence.

We are about to witness the acceleration of scientific progress on a scale for which we have no frame of reference. Dean was explicit: "You will get both a higher quantity and a higher quality of experiments, and that will lead to scientific breakthroughs and advances." The quantity is obvious. Thousands of parallel experimental loops churning constantly will make more raw attempts than any human lab could manage. But it's the quality part I have a hard time wrapping my head around. These aren't random attempts; they're a learning search based on the results of past loops. It's not a poetic figure of speech that there's a "loop" in Discovery Loop. It's a wiring diagram. Models generate experiments. Experiments are conducted in labs. Results feed the next loop. Off we go again.

Vinyals acknowledged that the key challenge is getting models to produce truly novel ideas, but he didn't seem concerned. I think he shouldn't be. Language models are already impressively good at open-ended idea generation. They just need a bridge back to experimental validation in the physical world. That's a hardware/robotics problem. It's not a computer science one. The underlying models, as far as I can tell, are sorted. Not finished. Sorted in the sense that what's left to do is wire things up, not make them smarter.

DeepMind shuffling its management structure for this is the other sign of where we are. Demis Hassabis, the co-founder and CEO, has left daily ops to become chair and Alphabet's chief scientist. Koray Kavukcuoglu is the new Senior VP of Google DeepMind, running Gemini. When you're moving a founding CEO off daily ops, that usually means they've got something bigger to work on than just the business as is. I think the takeaway is that Hassabis wanted to get back in the lab. What does he think is worth abandoning the corner office for? I'd like to hear.

I've thought for some time now that the critical path to transformative AI is going to involve a language model that's good enough to actually participate in AI research, plus a loop where it can iterate on itself. Discovery Loop is specifically created for that, and it's built on Gemini and Flamingo, plus more than a decade of cooperation between Dean, Ghemawat and Le. This is not some spare-time activity.

It was important when the White House set the national labs on this goal. Now it's imminent, and the folks who created the underlying tech are sprinting towards it themselves. All the other competitors in the field are going to have to follow, because if Alphabet has a loop, no research lab can afford to be without one. Six months ago that was a hypothetical. As of Wednesday it's an investment thesis with Kleiner, Khosla and Doerr attached. I'm giving it a year before it's just how ML systems are built, because the day after a Discovery Loop model invents something important, this will be an all-out race.

I said capability is outstripping evaluation, evaluation is outstripping oversight, and the 2027 timeline is getting squeezed. And now we just lost a year on all three of those sentences. The only way you win an iterated game is not to play it, and you can't opt out of this game. So on August 5 you won't think of it as a milestone. Those dates never feel like one in the moment. But it will be the date you look back on and say: see, that's when it started.
