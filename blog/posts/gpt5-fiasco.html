The GPT-5 Fiasco: How Sam Altman's Hubris Turned a Cost-Saving Measure Into Tech's Most Humiliating Debut

August 8, 2025

Just two days ago, Sam Altman was on camera promising GPT-5 would be the "best model in the world," calling it a "significant step toward AGI." What we got instead was one of the most embarrassing product launches in recent memory. Ridiculously bad charts. A routing system so broken it made the new model look less smart than its predecessor. Users raging so much that within a day OpenAI was forced to bring back GPT-4o.

I've been obsessively tracking AI progress since ChatGPT's launch, and I've seen my share of overhyped launches. But GPT-5 feels different. It's not just another letdown, it's showing how desperate OpenAI is to keep their exponential AI progress narrative going, while progress in reality is at most incremental.

The charts were the most embarrassing part. On the livestream OpenAI showed charts so deceptive they immediately spawned a Twitter meme called "chart crime." One chart showed GPT-5 with a 50% deception rate vs o3's 47.4% (the same number for all practical purposes), with o3's bar way taller to make it look like GPT-5 is better. Another compared scores of 74.9, 69.1 and 30.8 with bars that had no connection to the actual numbers.

Sam Altman called it a "mega chart screwup" on Twitter, but it was too late. Nobody missed the irony that the supposedly premier AI company could not even get charts right for their own event. Ex-OpenAI researcher Nat McAleese (now at Anthropic) smugly remarked that people kept texting him that he "would never have let that plot happen" back when he was their "chart crime police."

But the chart mess was only the start. Their headline feature for GPT-5 - the "unified" experience with a smart router that automatically picks the best model to use - completely crashed at launch. Reddit was flooded with users complaining that GPT-5 felt "dumber" than GPT-4o. And finally, on Friday during a damage control AMA, Altman admitted that the router "was out of commission for a chunk of the day" due to what he delicately called a "sev."

So the supposedly most advanced model ever was randomly routing your query to whatever model was still up and running, making it seem weaker than the last one. Maybe if your big new thing is broken automation that makes your product worse, you're trying too hard?

The routing mess is a revealing glimpse into what GPT-5 actually is: cost savings disguised as technological advancement. The "unified" model isn't meant to improve user experience. It's OpenAI's way to dramatically cut their compute costs by routing most queries to lower-cost (smaller) models while giving users the impression they're getting the best AI around.

Look at the context window situation to see what I mean. GPT-5 in the API can use 400,000 tokens. But ChatGPT users get a fraction of that. Free users dropped from 32,000 tokens down to 8,000! Plus users went from 128,000 tokens down to 32,000. Meanwhile, Claude gives everyone 200,000 tokens. And Gemini gives you a million! OpenAI literally made most people's experience worse, but claimed they're giving you the "best model in the world."

Users have been furious about this. They're saying GPT-5 feels "corporate" and "sanitized" - more like talking to a customer service bot than an AI. So much hate, in fact, that during the AMA users were begging for GPT-4o to be permanently brought back. Altman had to promise they were "looking into" it. If you have to immediately give people access to the previous version for your product launch, you've got a problem.

Through all of this chaos, Altman's hubris has been on full display. His grandiose claims about GPT-5 being "unimaginable at any previous time in history" ring hollow when the product is struggling with basics like routing. His recent podcast appearances where he talks about feeling "useless" next to GPT-5 come across as performance art, not genuine reflection on what the technology actually delivers.

Even the prediction markets have picked up on it. Despite all the hype, Google has now overtaken OpenAI in bets on which company will have the top model by the end of August. People in the AI community are evidently starting to lose confidence in OpenAI's direction.

For someone my age starting college at a time when AI is supposed to be changing everything, GPT-5's launch is a grim reminder that most of the advancements in AI are incremental, costly and often oversold. There's huge pressure on the firms that build these things to keep up growth stories that may not match what's happening technically.

If you ignore the marketing buzzwords, the real innovation of GPT-5 is clear. It's algorithmic penny pinching dressed up as an improvement to user experience. The reason for the router isn't convenience. It's cost management. The reason for the reduction in context windows isn't quality. It's economics. And even the increased safety measures serve a financial purpose, by reducing the need for human oversight.

That's why GPT-5's pricing is "aggressively competitive." OpenAI is able to undercut its competitors because most of the time, you're not actually using their most expensive model. It's a race to the bottom dressed up as a race to the top.

GPT-5 will not be remembered for its technical accomplishments. It will be remembered as the moment one of the most important firms in AI put marketing ahead of honesty. Cost-cutting ahead of capability. Ego ahead of evidence. In trying to prove they're still ahead of the curve, OpenAI may have revealed they're actually behind it.

And you know what? Maybe that's what we needed to see.
